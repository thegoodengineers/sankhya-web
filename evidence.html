03 · The evidence

Every number was produced by a command, and the CSV is named.

docs/BENCHMARKS.md is generated from bench/results/ by a script, so it cannot drift. Each CSV records the instance, its SHA-256, our objective, the published value, the gap, status, wall time, iterations, the commit and the machine.

The full tables, every instance named, are on the generated Benchmarks page.

Mittelmann is where the solver stops today: 3 of 8 across the three engines, 2 of 8 under the default selection, on a 96 GB node (12-vCPU EPYC 9555). brazil3 (interior point, 1.9 s) and chromaticindex1024-7 (first-order, 21 s) finish under auto; the first-order engine alone adds qap15 in 93.5 s; the interior point finishes brazil3 and qap15 and declines on the other six - not for memory but on its ordering budget and first factorization, named row by row in issue #576. Every finish is verified independently and agrees with HiGHS. GPU: the CUDA port of PDHG, on one laptop card, loses below about 10,000 rows (0.55× at 5,000) and wins at 10,000 × 10,000 by 1.37x at 1e-4 and 1.54× at 1e-8 in the later run, 1.63× and 1.51× in the earlier one - two runs, both named, no single figure quoted alone.4 On real models, fresh clone at 58a8374, against the faster of the two CPU arms: on the L4 the 16-thread arm is slower than one thread, so brazil3 is 5.75x at 1e-4 and 8.10x at 1e-8 and chromaticindex1024-7 2.92x / 2.88x, all against one thread; on an A100 brazil3 8.91x / 10.53x and chromaticindex1024-7 2.38x / 1.77x (gpu-real-l4-58a8374.csv, gpu-real-a100-58a8374.csv). At 1e-8 on brazil3 the GPU's answer is further from HiGHS than the CPU's, and on the 779,640-row refinery year every arm on both cards hits the 300 s limit. H100 not yet. Where the GPU loses on accuracy: at 1e-8 on brazil3 its answer is further from HiGHS than the CPU's, a relative gap of 2.4e-07 on the L4 and 1.3e-08 on the A100 against 1.2e-09 for both CPU arms, and that gap moves from run to run (9.15e-11 in the L4's median-of-three run, gpu-datacenter-l4-58a8374.csv, whose 1e-8 speed-up is 5.98x rather than 8.10x). On the refinery year the card is only further along: a gap of 3.7e-11 on the L4 and 5.5e-11 on the A100 against 3.6e-10 and 1.6e-08 for the better CPU arm. Measured beside it at 58a8374, all opt-in and off by default: cuDSS for the interior point's normal equations (#489, ipm-cudss-ab-{mittelmann,netlib}-{cpu,cpu-supernodal,cudss}-58a8374.csv) takes qap15 from the 300 s limit (scalar CPU factor) and 185.1 s (supernodal) to 18.9 s, solves rmine15 in 137.4 s where neither CPU factor does, and is slower than the scalar CPU factor on the smallest models (80bau3b 2.3 s against 1.2 s); two A100s over NVLink (#295, multi-gpu-e2e-2xA100-SXM4-40GB-NV12-58a8374.csv) are 1.64x faster per step at 4,000,000 rows, but the 2,000-step solve takes the same time (106.5 s against 108.4 s) because setup and host-side evaluation dominate, and two cards lose per step on the smaller Linf_520c (0.85x) and bdry2 (0.44x); root domain propagation on the device (#510, gpu-domain-prop-58a8374.csv) loses at every size, from 1422x slower at 1,000 rows to 1.13x slower at 1,000,000; Feasibility Jump on the device (#508, gpu-fj-ab-58a8374-e2e-L4.csv) finds a feasible point on 14 of 20 MIPLIB instances in 60 s against the CPU's 16 of 20 on the L4, and 15 of 20 against 16 on a V100 at ad57c03 (gpu-fj-ab-ad57c03-e2e-V100.csv), with the better objective on 14 of the 15 both find but its first point later on 14 of them; deterministic device reductions (#478) cost 2.2% per PDHG iteration on the refinery year on that V100 with three sparse products and nothing measurable with two (pdhg-478-refinery-*-ad57c03.csv); batched PDHG for node bounds and strong branching (#520, miplib-t2-520-*.csv against miplib-t2-509-off.csv) loses to leaving it off in every leg, with a weaker dual bound on 11 to 14 of the 20 instances.7 Robustness: exact rank checked over the rationals on a degenerate model, condition number 2.1e+30 with the objective provably unmoved, a 33.8% integrality gap closed.3

Reproduce

One command regenerates this page's numbers.

git clone https://github.com/thegoodengineers/SANKHYA
cd SANKHYA
scripts/reproduce.sh

Builds, runs the 1347 tests, the Netlib benchmark with verification, the HiGHS comparison and the PS26119 walkthrough, offline. Any step that cannot run on your machine says why and is listed again at the end, so a shorter run is never mistaken for a passing one. Commit hashes in the CSV names identify the exact solver that produced each number.
