Gaps

Eight things, with where each stands.

designed and builtpartly there
  • GPU acceleration · run on a laptop RTX 5050 (1.37x at 10,000x10,000), a datacenter NVIDIA L4 (4.21x there; brazil3 5.75x / 8.10x at 1e-4 / 1e-8 against one CPU thread, which beat 16) and an A100 (brazil3 8.91x / 10.53x against the faster CPU arm), with the GPU's answer further from HiGHS than the CPU's at 1e-8 (gpu-real-l4-58a8374.csv, gpu-real-a100-58a8374.csv; BENCHMARKS.md 1g and 1g.3). H100 not yet. --gpu falls back to the CPU on any device failure.
  • Speed at Mittelmann size · 3 of 8 across the three LP engines, 2 of 8 under auto, at 300 s on a 96 GB node (mittelmann-5c7efbc.csv and its pdhg and ipm companions). qap15 is solved by PDHG and by the interior point alone but not under auto; the interior point finishes 2 and declines on the other six on its ordering budget and first factorization, not for memory; the dual simplex's cost per iteration above 20,000 rows is the open line; all three levers are pulled (row-wise gather, push-form transposed solve, a Forrest-Tomlin update that stays opt-in after its stability tests) and the next one is the ratio test.
  • Branch-and-cut proper · MIR cuts with row aggregation, cut rounds below the root with an aged pool, clique and (0, 0.5)-Chvátal-Gomory cuts, conflict analysis and ten primal heuristic switches landed (the mip_heur_* options: Feasibility Jump, lock rounding, the repair search, the feasibility pump, RINS, RENS, and fractional, coefficient, vector-length and guided diving), each gated by the exact-oracle or enumeration sweep. Only the fractional dive is on by default, with rounding at every node running either way. Measured clean on MIPLIB at 60 s with cut selection landed, the root round proves 10 where no cuts prove 9 at 1.015x the nodes, and is on by default; rounds below the root lose that proof again and stay opt-in; the heuristics and conflicts are off until a clean A/B on main says what they buy.
  • Crossover · landed: the interior point's answer is pushed to a vertex by default, cheap on Netlib (median 41 pivots on the small set); on large degenerate models the pivots still cost as much as a cold simplex, the primal-push phase is the open line.
  • Warm start through the API · landed: edit a bound or a price in place and re-solve from the previous basis, from C or Python; a tightened bound re-solves in under a fifth of the cold pivots on the models tested.
  • NLP and MINLP · a local interior point for smooth NLP and NLP-based branch and bound for convex MINLP, measured on main at ad57c03: 64 of 70 Hock-Schittkowski at the published optimum and 70 of 70 accepted by the independent checker (51 locally_optimal, 19 optimal), 14 of 17 convex MINLPLib proved optimal (nlp-hs-ad57c03.csv, nlp-minlplib-ad57c03.csv). Local means local: six HS problems end at another local optimum, jit1 ends numerical_error, and non-convex MINLP is refused.
  • Engine selection · auto is a rule table on rows and nonzeros, every threshold tied to a CSV; the answer carries the rule and the reason, and a declined interior point hands the rest of the budget to PDHG or the dual simplex.
  • Parallel tree search · column loops run under OpenMP, bit-identical; since #403 the branch-and-bound tree runs on mip_threads workers, one thread by default. Four threads reached and proved the same MIPLIB instances at 60 s on the branch and were slower on the small proofs; no A/B on main is committed yet, so the default stays one.
Where this is going

Four phases, from the open issues.

Taken from the issues carrying the finale label in the tracker and the roadmap in #540. New behaviour ships off by default until it passes a clean A/B on main.

  1. Numerical hardening.

    Make every answer checkable where it is not yet: the QP answers the verifier rejects (#590), exported Farkas rays (#559), MILP proofs and safe dual bounds measured on MIPLIB (#518, #519), PDHG infeasibility detection (#484).

  2. A faster MILP tree and QP engines.

    Stronger cuts and presolve (#498, #511, #512), conflict analysis (#503), no model copy per node (#501), and the QP interior point and an MIQP node solver through their A/B (#490, #494).

  3. GPU runs at scale, with a full benchmark report.

    A device-resident PDHG loop (#478), batched PDHG nodes (#520), three-seed MIPLIB (#504), and a regenerated BENCHMARKS.md after a three-seed A/B of every off-by-default feature.

  4. An industrial pilot on refinery models.

    Pooling benchmarks (#516), warm re-solve over a rolling horizon (#524), decomposition (#525), a planner's report (#527) and convex MINLP (#528).

And what it is

An engine, three ways to call it, and the evidence beside it.

No GUI. The thing a refinery's planning tools would link against, plus the proof that it is right.

Evidenceevery CSV, the generated benchmarks page, provenance, SPDX SBOM, one-command reproduction
VerifierPython, shares no code, proves certificates; the pass rates are counted by it
Python bindingsctypes over the C API, nothing to compile
C APIFFI-safe, callbacks, interruption, the same options table
CLIMPS / LP / QPS in, solution file with prices, ranges, certificates and IIS out
Solver corestatic library: presolve, scaling, the LP engines (dual and primal simplex, interior point, PDHG), the QP engines (Condat-Vũ, proximal interior point), branch and cut over LP and QP; no linked library is an optimization solver
GPU backendCUDA port of the first-order engine, behind a build flag, compiled in CI; measured on three cards, the CSVs named
The GPU plan

What happened the day the hardware arrived.

Written down before the card existed, in docs/GPU_PLAN.md, and then followed on a rented L4 on 23 September 2026: build from a fresh clone, warm-up solve, the three runners, the CSVs stamped with the commit.

Day 0Intake: driver, nvcc, device probe, a CPU baseline re-measured on that machine, alone.
Day 1The plumbing: CMake guard, runtime --gpu flag, fallback path. Done on main; CPU numbers did not move.
Day 2The kernels, reviewed on the device: agreement with the CPU engine to 1e-9 on nine instances.
Day 3The evidence: CPU-versus-GPU crossover by model size, committed as a CSV, losing region included. Done on the laptop card, again on the L4, and on an A100 against both CPU arms at 58a8374; the first L4 run was thrown away because its tree was dirty.
Day 4Documents, the demo section, the final read against the problem statement.

The claim is not a speed record. It is "a from-scratch GPU first-order engine, verified, with its crossover measured and published on three cards". That is defensible in front of a judge because every figure is a CSV: 4.21x on an L4's synthetic ladder, 10.53x on an A100 on brazil3 at 1e-8 with the GPU's larger error there stated beside it, and the options that lose named as losing.
