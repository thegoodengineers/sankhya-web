02 · The solver

One call, seven engines, and a checker that trusts none of them.

solve(const Model&, const Options&) → Solution is the one entry point. Everything below plugs into it.

Presolve's postsolve re-measures the recovered point against the original model, so a wrong dual surfaces as a violation rather than as a quiet wrong number.
Engines

Chosen by the problem class.

Four for LP (dual simplex, primal simplex, interior point, PDHG), two for convex QP (first-order Condat-Vũ, proximal interior point) and branch and cut for MILP and MIQP: the list sankhya engines prints at 493fa1d. A CUDA build adds the GPU PDHG.

LP · default below 20,000 rows

Dual simplex

Bounded-variable revised simplex, sparse Markowitz LU, Devex pricing. Produces a basis: the node engine for branch and bound. auto picks it by a rule table whose every threshold names its CSV.

LP · primal

Primal simplex

Composite phase 1, no big-M. The hand-over engine when a basis needs repair.

LP · auto from 20,000 rows or 100,000 nonzeros

Interior point

Mehrotra predictor-corrector through a from-scratch sparse LDLᵀ with AMD ordering, started from Mehrotra's shifted point. On its own it verifies 73 of 89 Netlib instances (51 before the new start, 69 before it learned to raise its regularization when a Newton direction comes back non-finite), and when the barrier vanishes it takes one more step on a stronger diagonal, which turns the 20,000-row staircase from a feasible point into a proof that crossover finishes in 11 seconds. Its answer is pushed to a vertex by crossover, so it carries a basis; a set-up past a fifth of the time limit declines, and another engine takes the rest.

LP · first-order, auto from 100,000 rows

Restarted PDHG

Two sparse mat-vecs and a projection per iteration: the GPU shape. Finished by the interior point from its own point. The CUDA port compiles in CI and has run on three cards. On an RTX 5050 Laptop GPU, below about 10,000 rows the kernel-launch overhead costs more than the parallelism saves (0.55x at 5,000x5,000); at 10,000x10,000 the GPU wins by 1.37x at 1e-4 and 1.54x at 1e-8 (gpu-fb72ab4.csv), and an earlier run of the same protocol read 1.63x and 1.51x (gpu-a4f02b1.csv) - two runs, both named, which is why no single figure is quoted alone. On a datacenter NVIDIA L4 (E2E TIR, fresh clone at fdc89c5) the same protocol reads 4.21x at 1e-4 and 3.90x at 1e-8 at 10,000x10,000 against that host's CPU on one thread, 2.05x at 5,000 and still a loss at 2,000 (gpu-l4-fdc89c5.csv). On real models, fresh clone at 58a8374, against the faster of the two CPU arms: on the L4 the 16-thread arm is slower than one thread, so brazil3 is 5.75x at 1e-4 and 8.10x at 1e-8 and chromaticindex1024-7 2.92x / 2.88x, all against one thread; on an A100 brazil3 8.91x / 10.53x and chromaticindex1024-7 2.38x / 1.77x (gpu-real-l4-58a8374.csv, gpu-real-a100-58a8374.csv). At 1e-8 on brazil3 the GPU's answer is further from HiGHS than the CPU's, and on the 779,640-row refinery year every arm on both cards hits the 300 s limit. H100 not yet. Sections 1g and 1g.3 of BENCHMARKS.md.

MILP · MIQP

Branch and cut

Reliability branching, dive then best-bound, warm-started dual node LPs. Gomory, cover and mixed-integer rounding cuts at the root and in rounds below it, with an aged cut pool. The root round is on by default: with cut selection it proves 10 of 30 where no cuts prove 9, at 1.015x the nodes; rounds below the root lose that proof again and stay opt-in. Formulation symmetry rows are on by default since #717, and the run under them at ad57c03 reaches 14 of 30 and proves 11 (miplib-ad57c03.csv). The tree runs on mip_threads workers, one by default (#403). For MIQP the node bound comes from the first-order engine by default; the QP interior point as node solver (#687, miqp_node_ipm) is opt-in.

QP · the default

Condat-Vũ

Convexity decided by LDLᵀ before any arithmetic. A non-convex Hessian is refused, never reported as global. On QPLIB's convex continuous small tier at ad57c03 (qplib-small-ad57c03.csv, 1000 s) the default engine passes 6 of 11 and the interior point 7 of 11; one instance QPLIB tags convex is refused at a 7.8e-09 pivot, not yet checked.

QP · qp_algorithm=ipm

Proximal interior point

Mehrotra predictor-corrector on the regularized quasi-definite augmented system, sparse LDLᵀ (#490). Reached by name until an A/B on main says otherwise. On the same QPLIB tier it passes 7 of 11 against the default engine's 6.

What is new

Ten additions, each with its option, its default and its result.

Every item was confirmed on main at 493fa1d: the option in sankhya options, or the file named. A default here is a measurement, so most are off until a clean A/B on main. Where a committed run loses, the number is given.

engine_race · off

Verifier-gated engine race

Under auto, the dual simplex, the interior point with crossover and PDHG run at once, and an answer wins only after an in-process check against the original model passes: the KKT conditions the verifier checks for an optimum, the Farkas certificate for an infeasibility. A rejected answer neither wins nor stops the others. Mittelmann: 3 of 8 against auto's 2 on the same binary (mittelmann-race-65eecbc.csv, which adds qap15). Netlib: the same 89 of 89 verified and 81 matched, at 182.9 s of solver time in total (netlib-full-race-65eecbc.csv). Source: engine_race.cpp.

write_certificate · off

A checkable proof of a MILP optimum

Writes the search as a proof in the VIPR format (Cheung, Gleixner and Steffy, IPCO 2017), and verify_certificate.py checks it in exact rational arithmetic: every leaf bounded by the safe bound of its LP duals, every branching node combining its children. While it writes, presolve, node propagation and integer row tightening are off and the search runs on one thread; with symmetry or objective rows, after a restart, or for an MIQP nothing is written and the log says why. No committed result file yet.

safe_bounds · off

Safe dual bounds

Every node is pruned on a rigorous bound (Neumaier and Shcherbina, 2004): the node LP's row duals turned into a lower bound by weak duality with outward rounding, valid whatever the LP's tolerances did. The log reports the largest gap between the believed bound and the safe one. No committed result file yet.

exact · off

Exact rational re-check of an LP basis

After a plain LP reaches optimal, the reported basis is rebuilt in exact rational arithmetic (Gleixner, Steffy and Wolter, 2016). On realistic models it declines far more often than it verifies: at 493fa1d even the 3-row demo/crude_blend.mps declines, an intermediate value overflowing 128-bit integer arithmetic. No committed result file yet.

nonconvex=global · off (refuse)

Global optimum for bilinear pooling

Spatial branch and bound over McCormick relaxations, reporting optimal only when the proven bound closes the gap. By default a model with products of columns is refused rather than solved to a local point. Run at 493fa1d, demo/pooling_haverly.mps reaches 400, Haverly's published optimum, with status optimal, and the independent verifier passes all 9 of its checks. The pooling benchmark set is not yet run on main.

gpu_batch_nodes, gpu_feasibility_jump, gpu_pump, gpu_fix_and_prop · off

GPU node bounding and GPU heuristics

Feasibility Jump on the device finds a feasible point on 15 of 20 MIPLIB instances in 60 s against the CPU's 16 on a V100 (gpu-fj-ab-ad57c03-e2e-V100.csv), and 14 against 16 on an L4 (gpu-fj-ab-58a8374-e2e-L4.csv). On 20 MIPLIB tier-2 instances at 60 s no leg, off included, matches or proves a published optimum: batched node bounds leave a weaker dual bound than off on 11 of the 20 (miplib-t2-520-nodes.csv against miplib-t2-509-off.csv), and 11 to 14 with batched strong branching; the GPU pump finds an incumbent on 15 against off's 14, with a weaker bound on 9 (miplib-t2-509-pump-gpu.csv); GPU fix-and-propagate finds one on 14, the same as off (miplib-t2-509-fixprop-gpu.csv).

tools/bundle.py · tools/replay_bundle.py

Replayable evidence bundle

One zip with the model, the options, the binary's version and commit, the SBOM's hash, the full log, the solution and a manifest of SHA-256 sums. Replay re-hashes every file, so a tampered bundle fails, re-runs the verifier, and with --resolve solves again and compares. No committed result file yet.

tools/parametric.py · tools/repair_infeasibility.py

Parametric LP and infeasibility repair

Parametric LP walks one cost or right-hand side across its ranging breakpoints, warm-starting at each (parametric-crude-blend-AL-887b173.csv and parametric-crude-blend-THRUPUT-887b173.csv, three rows each). Repair finds the smallest weighted relaxation that makes an infeasible plan possible, by elastic programming (Chinneck, 2008). No committed result file for repair yet.

bindings/python/sankhya/adapters

PuLP, Pyomo and CVXPY adapters

A planner who already has a PuLP, Pyomo or CVXPY model changes one line, the solver call. No committed result file yet.

deterministic · off

Deterministic mode

No decision is taken on the clock: a wall-clock time limit is refused rather than ignored, and the column loops run on one thread unless threads is set. On brazil3 at 1e-8 on an L4, three default runs took 180,560, 215,400 and 184,680 iterations to three different objectives, and three deterministic runs took 138,240 each to the identical objective (gpu-run-to-run-58a8374.csv). Nothing is claimed across compilers or machines.

Verification

Two readers, two arithmetics.

The verifier reads the model file and the solution file and re-derives feasibility, duality, integrality and the gap. Infeasible carries a Farkas certificate; unbounded carries a ray; an IIS carries witnesses. All proved, not trusted.

Compared

Ahead and behind, on the same evidence.

Against HiGHS where a like-for-like run exists, and against the problem statement everywhere else.

Ahead

  • Independent checker on every answer: a separate program, sharing no code with the solver, re-derives each result from the model file and the solution file.
  • Provenance enforced in CI, not asserted in a README.
  • Scale evidence with exact optima: generators build the model backwards from a known KKT point.
  • A planner's solution file: prices, ranges, conflict set, progress stream.
  • An honest ledger shipped with the product.

Behind

  • Raw simplex speed: machine-dependent against HiGHS on the Netlib medium tier. On a rented 7-vCPU EPYC 7542 node at ad57c03 (compare-highs-medium-ad57c03.csv): total solve time 0.99x HiGHS's, shifted geometric mean 1.0x, median 1.02x, slower on 27 of 50 instances, and 31 of the 50 timing envelopes overlap. On a laptop at bf3df02 the per-instance median read 2.01x to 2.12x over three runs. Both readings stay in the tree; neither is quoted alone.
  • MILP proofs: 11 of 30 on MIPLIB, 14 reached, at 60 s on main at ad57c03 with root cuts and symmetry rows on by default (miplib-ad57c03.csv); over three seeds only 9 are proved on every seed and b-ball, enlight8 and neos-3611689-kaihu change verdict with the seed (summary-miplib-seeds3-ad57c03.csv); tree rounds exist and were measured to lose a proof again in fewer, dearer nodes; the tree runs on mip_threads workers, one by default (#403).
  • Very large LPs: Mittelmann 2 of 8 inside 300 s under auto, 3 of 8 across the three LP engines.
  • GPU crossover, not dominance: GPU PDHG wins at 10,000x10,000 - 1.37x at 1e-4 and 1.54x at 1e-8 in gpu-fb72ab4.csv, 1.63x and 1.51x in the earlier gpu-a4f02b1.csv - and loses below the crossover (0.55x at 5,000). On a datacenter L4 the crossover is the same shape and the win is 4.21x at 10,000x10,000 (gpu-l4-fdc89c5.csv, one CPU thread). On brazil3 against the faster CPU arm: 5.75x / 8.10x at 1e-4 / 1e-8 on the L4 and 8.91x / 10.53x on an A100 (gpu-real-l4-58a8374.csv, gpu-real-a100-58a8374.csv). Where the GPU loses on accuracy: at 1e-8 on brazil3 its answer is further from HiGHS than the CPU's, a relative gap of 2.4e-07 on the L4 and 1.3e-08 on the A100 against 1.2e-09 for both CPU arms, and that gap moves from run to run (9.15e-11 in the L4's median-of-three run, gpu-datacenter-l4-58a8374.csv, whose 1e-8 speed-up is 5.98x rather than 8.10x). On the refinery year the card is only further along: a gap of 3.7e-11 on the L4 and 5.5e-11 on the A100 against 3.6e-10 and 1.6e-08 for the better CPU arm. Since then, not yet re-measured on main: the convergence evaluation runs on the card by default, one GPU (#711) and several (#713), host evaluation kept as the reference. Measured beside it at 58a8374, each opt-in and off by default: cuDSS for the interior point's normal equations (#489, ipm_linear_solver=cudss; cuDSS by default in a cuDSS build is PR #722, pending;ipm-cudss-ab-{mittelmann,netlib}-{cpu,cpu-supernodal,cudss}-58a8374.csv) takes qap15 from the 300 s limit (scalar CPU factor) and 185.1 s (supernodal) to 18.9 s, solves rmine15 in 137.4 s where neither CPU factor does, and is slower than the scalar CPU factor on the smallest models (80bau3b 2.3 s against 1.2 s); two A100s over NVLink (#295, multi-gpu-e2e-2xA100-SXM4-40GB-NV12-58a8374.csv) are 1.64x faster per step at 4,000,000 rows, but the 2,000-step solve took the same time (106.5 s against 108.4 s) because setup and host-side evaluation dominated at that commit, and two cards lose per step on the smaller Linf_520c (0.85x) and bdry2 (0.44x); root domain propagation on the device (#510, gpu-domain-prop-58a8374.csv) loses at every size, from 1422x slower at 1,000 rows to 1.13x slower at 1,000,000; Feasibility Jump on the device (#508, gpu-fj-ab-58a8374-e2e-L4.csv) finds a feasible point on 14 of 20 MIPLIB instances in 60 s against the CPU's 16 of 20 on the L4, and 15 of 20 against 16 on a V100 at ad57c03 (gpu-fj-ab-ad57c03-e2e-V100.csv), with the better objective on 14 of the 15 both find but its first point later on 14 of them; deterministic device reductions (#478) cost 2.2% per PDHG iteration on the refinery year on that V100 with three sparse products and nothing measurable with two (pdhg-478-refinery-*-ad57c03.csv); batched PDHG for node bounds and strong branching (#520, miplib-t2-520-*.csv against miplib-t2-509-off.csv) loses to leaving it off in every leg, with a weaker dual bound on 11 to 14 of the 20 instances. H100 not yet.
