02 · The solver

Four engines behind one call, and a checker that trusts none of them.

solve(const Model&, const Options&) → Solution is the one entry point. Everything below plugs into it.

Presolve's postsolve re-measures the recovered point against the original model, so a wrong dual surfaces as a violation rather than as a quiet wrong number.
Engines

Chosen by the problem class.

LP · default below 20,000 rows

Dual simplex

Bounded-variable revised simplex, sparse Markowitz LU, Devex pricing. Produces a basis: the node engine for branch and bound. auto picks it by a rule table whose every threshold names its CSV.

LP · primal

Primal simplex

Composite phase 1, no big-M. The hand-over engine when a basis needs repair.

LP · auto from 20,000 rows or 100,000 nonzeros

Interior point

Mehrotra predictor-corrector through a from-scratch sparse LDLᵀ with AMD ordering, started from Mehrotra's shifted point. On its own it verifies 73 of 89 Netlib instances (51 before the new start, 69 before it learned to raise its regularization when a Newton direction comes back non-finite), and when the barrier vanishes it takes one more step on a stronger diagonal, which turns the 20,000-row staircase from a feasible point into a proof that crossover finishes in 11 seconds. Its answer is pushed to a vertex by crossover, so it carries a basis; a set-up past a fifth of the time limit declines, and another engine takes the rest.

LP · first-order, auto from 100,000 rows

Restarted PDHG

Two sparse mat-vecs and a projection per iteration: the GPU shape. Finished by the interior point from its own point. The CUDA port compiles in CI and has run on one card, an RTX 5050 Laptop GPU: below about 10,000 rows the kernel-launch overhead costs more than the parallelism saves (0.55x at 5,000x5,000); at 10,000x10,000 the GPU wins by 1.37x at 1e-4 and 1.54x at 1e-8 (gpu-fb72ab4.csv), and an earlier run of the same protocol read 1.63x and 1.51x (gpu-a4f02b1.csv) - two runs, both named, which is why no single figure is quoted alone. On a datacenter NVIDIA L4 (E2E TIR, fresh clone at fdc89c5) the same protocol reads 4.21x at 1e-4 and 3.90x at 1e-8 at 10,000x10,000 against that host's CPU on one thread, 2.05x at 5,000 and still a loss at 2,000 (gpu-l4-fdc89c5.csv). On real models, fresh clone at 58a8374, against the faster of the two CPU arms: on the L4 the 16-thread arm is slower than one thread, so brazil3 is 5.75x at 1e-4 and 8.10x at 1e-8 and chromaticindex1024-7 2.92x / 2.88x, all against one thread; on an A100 brazil3 8.91x / 10.53x and chromaticindex1024-7 2.38x / 1.77x (gpu-real-l4-58a8374.csv, gpu-real-a100-58a8374.csv). At 1e-8 on brazil3 the GPU's answer is further from HiGHS than the CPU's, and on the 779,640-row refinery year every arm on both cards hits the 300 s limit. H100 not yet. Sections 1g and 1g.3 of BENCHMARKS.md.

MILP · MIQP

Branch and cut

Reliability branching, dive then best-bound, warm-started dual node LPs. Gomory, cover and mixed-integer rounding cuts at the root and in rounds below it, with an aged cut pool. The root round is on by default: with cut selection it proves 10 of 30 where no cuts prove 9, at 1.015x the nodes; rounds below the root lose that proof again and stay opt-in.

QP

Condat-Vũ

Convexity decided by LDLᵀ before any arithmetic. A non-convex Hessian is refused, never reported as global.

Verification

Two readers, two arithmetics.

The verifier reads the model file and the solution file and re-derives feasibility, duality, integrality and the gap. Infeasible carries a Farkas certificate; unbounded carries a ray; an IIS carries witnesses. All proved, not trusted.

Compared

Ahead and behind, on the same evidence.

Three kinds of solver exist: open-source simplex codes, commercial ones, GPU first-order codes.

Ahead

  • Independent checker on every answer. No open-source solver ships one; the commercial ones ship none.
  • Provenance enforced in CI, not asserted in a README.
  • Scale evidence with exact optima: generators build the model backwards from a known KKT point.
  • A planner's solution file: prices, ranges, conflict set, progress stream.
  • An honest ledger shipped with the product.

Behind

  • Raw simplex speed: about 2x HiGHS's wall time on the Netlib medium tier.
  • MILP proofs: 10 of 30 on MIPLIB, 13 reached, with root cuts on by default; tree rounds exist and were measured to lose that proof again in fewer, dearer nodes; one core in the tree.
  • Very large LPs: Mittelmann 2 of 8 inside 300 s under auto, 3 of 8 across the three engines.
  • GPU crossover, not dominance: GPU PDHG wins at 10,000x10,000 - 1.37x at 1e-4 and 1.54x at 1e-8 in gpu-fb72ab4.csv, 1.63x and 1.51x in the earlier gpu-a4f02b1.csv - and loses below the crossover (0.55x at 5,000). On a datacenter L4 the crossover is the same shape and the win is 4.21x at 10,000x10,000 (gpu-l4-fdc89c5.csv, one CPU thread). On brazil3 against the faster CPU arm: 5.75x / 8.10x at 1e-4 / 1e-8 on the L4 and 8.91x / 10.53x on an A100 (gpu-real-l4-58a8374.csv, gpu-real-a100-58a8374.csv). Where the GPU loses on accuracy: at 1e-8 on brazil3 its answer is further from HiGHS than the CPU's, a relative gap of 2.4e-07 on the L4 and 1.3e-08 on the A100 against 1.2e-09 for both CPU arms, and that gap moves from run to run (9.15e-11 in the L4's median-of-three run, gpu-datacenter-l4-58a8374.csv, whose 1e-8 speed-up is 5.98x rather than 8.10x). On the refinery year the card is only further along: a gap of 3.7e-11 on the L4 and 5.5e-11 on the A100 against 3.6e-10 and 1.6e-08 for the better CPU arm. Measured beside it at 58a8374, all opt-in and off by default: cuDSS for the interior point's normal equations (#489, ipm-cudss-ab-{mittelmann,netlib}-{cpu,cpu-supernodal,cudss}-58a8374.csv) takes qap15 from the 300 s limit (scalar CPU factor) and 185.1 s (supernodal) to 18.9 s, solves rmine15 in 137.4 s where neither CPU factor does, and is slower than the scalar CPU factor on the smallest models (80bau3b 2.3 s against 1.2 s); two A100s over NVLink (#295, multi-gpu-e2e-2xA100-SXM4-40GB-NV12-58a8374.csv) are 1.64x faster per step at 4,000,000 rows, but the 2,000-step solve takes the same time (106.5 s against 108.4 s) because setup and host-side evaluation dominate, and two cards lose per step on the smaller Linf_520c (0.85x) and bdry2 (0.44x); root domain propagation on the device (#510, gpu-domain-prop-58a8374.csv) loses at every size, from 1422x slower at 1,000 rows to 1.13x slower at 1,000,000; Feasibility Jump on the device (#508, gpu-fj-ab-58a8374-e2e-L4.csv) finds a feasible point on 14 of 20 MIPLIB instances in 60 s against the CPU's 16 of 20; batched PDHG for node bounds and strong branching (#520, miplib-t2-520-*.csv against miplib-t2-509-off.csv) loses to leaving it off in every leg, with a weaker dual bound on 11 to 14 of the 20 instances. H100 not yet.
