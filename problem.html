01 · The problem

A refinery asked for a sovereign alternative to CPLEX and Xpress.

Problem statement SIH26119, Mangalore Refinery and Petrochemicals Ltd. Below: what a refinery model looks like, what the statement demands, and why "not built on another solver" is the hard part.

The model

What a refinery solves every week.

Crude choice, unit throughput and pool blending under quality and delivery constraints. This shape is demo/crude_blend.mps at small size and the generated refinery year at 779,640 rows.
Why it matters

Three reasons this is not a toy.

Constant re-planning

Crude purchases, unit runs and blends are re-decided all the time. Each decision is an LP or MILP.

Licensed and opaque

Today's answers come from foreign software nobody in the plant can inspect, audit or extend.

Wrong answers are silent

A plan that violates a spec by one part in a thousand does not crash. It ships a bad blend.

The statement

What SIH26119 asks for, against what exists on main.

From the coverage tracker in the repository, checked against the source.

donepartial
  • Linear programming · revised simplex, primal and dual
  • Mixed-integer LP · branch and bound, reliability branching
  • Convex QP and MIQP · a proximal interior point by default, Condat-Vũ its fallback; 106 of 138 Maros–Mészáros; non-convex refused with a certificate
  • NLP / MINLP · local interior point, convex MINLP branch and bound: 64 of 70 Hock-Schittkowski, 14 of 17 convex MINLPLib, every answer checked
  • First-order method · restarted PDHG, finished by the interior point
  • Interior point · Mehrotra, sparse LDLᵀ; auto chooses it from 20,000 rows or 100,000 nonzeros
  • Presolve, cutting planes, heuristics · 8 presolve reductions on by default and 8 more built and off; the root cut round on by default, rounds below the root off; rounding and the fractional dive on, the other heuristics off until their A/B
  • Sensitivity ranging · cost and right-hand side, degeneracy stated
  • Sparse linear algebra · Markowitz LU, hyper-sparse solves, LDLᵀ
  • Multi-core · OpenMP column loops, bit-identical at any thread count; the branch-and-bound tree on mip_threads workers, one by default
  • GPU acceleration · CUDA backend on main, run on three cards. Laptop RTX 5050: loses below ~10,000 rows, 1.37x-1.63x at 10,000x10,000 across two named runs. Datacenter NVIDIA L4 (E2E TIR, fresh clone at fdc89c5, gpu-l4-fdc89c5.csv): 4.21x at 10,000x10,000 at 1e-4, 3.90x at 1e-8, against that host's CPU on one thread (this synthetic ladder has not been re-run with the multithreaded arm). On real models, fresh clone at 58a8374, against the faster of the two CPU arms: on the L4 the 16-thread arm is slower than one thread, so brazil3 is 5.75x at 1e-4 and 8.10x at 1e-8 and chromaticindex1024-7 2.92x / 2.88x, all against one thread; on an A100 brazil3 8.91x / 10.53x and chromaticindex1024-7 2.38x / 1.77x (gpu-real-l4-58a8374.csv, gpu-real-a100-58a8374.csv). At 1e-8 on brazil3 the GPU's answer is further from HiGHS than the CPU's, and on the 779,640-row refinery year every arm on both cards hits the 300 s limit. H100 not yet.
