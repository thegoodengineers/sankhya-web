01 · The problem statement

A refinery asked for a sovereign alternative to CPLEX and Xpress.

PS SIH26119 asks for an indigenous, GPU-accelerated optimization solver: LP, MILP and QP as the core, modular enough to take NLP and MINLP later, built on revised simplex, first-order methods, interior point and branch-and-bound, with presolve, cutting planes, sensitivity analysis and heuristics. It has to handle the models a refinery actually runs, crude blending, unit scheduling, production planning, and it has to be benchmarked on Netlib, MIPLIB and Mittelmann against an established solver. Above all, it must not be a wrapper around an existing open-source solver.

Why it matters to MRPL

A refinery re-plans constantly: which crudes to buy, how to run the distillation units, what to blend into which pool to meet a sulphur specification at the lowest cost. Each of those is a linear or mixed-integer program, and today the answers come from licensed foreign software whose internals nobody in the plant can inspect.

What "sovereign" has to mean

Not a rebranded HiGHS. Every algorithm written from the paper or the textbook it comes from, with the citation in the source above the implementation, a dependency table that explains why each library is not a solver, and a bill of materials that CI regenerates and checks.

What a wrong answer costs

Numerical code fails silently. A solver that returns a plausible plan which violates a constraint by one part in a thousand does not crash; it ships a bad blend. So the project is organized around verification first and speed second, and says so.

02 · What exists, and what is different

There are three kinds of solver already. This is measured against each.

The open-source simplex codes (HiGHS, CBC, GLPK), the commercial ones (CPLEX, Gurobi, Xpress), and the GPU first-order codes (PDLP, cuPDLP). Where SANKHYA is ahead is on evidence in the repository; where it is behind is on the same evidence, stated in the same place.

What it has that they do not

  • An independent checker on every answer. tools/verify_solution.py reads the model and the solution file and re-derives feasibility, duality, integrality and the gap. An infeasible verdict carries a Farkas certificate the checker proves; an unbounded one carries a ray; an irreducible infeasible subsystem carries its own certificate and one witness per element. No open-source solver ships a checker of its own output; the commercial ones ship none.
  • Provenance that is enforced. A CI job dumps the link line and fails the build if a solver library appears in the binary. "Not built on another solver" is a checked property, not a sentence.
  • Scale evidence with exact optima. Three committed generators build a million-row LP backwards from a known KKT point, so a run at that size is an accuracy check rather than a timing loop. Public benchmarks have no such families.
  • The solution file a planner reads. Shadow prices, sensitivity ranges with the basis's degeneracy stated, the smallest conflicting set of constraints when there is no plan, and a progress stream an operator can tail during a long solve.
  • An honest ledger shipped with the product. The README's gaps table, the demo's last section and the coverage tracker say what is missing, with the number.

What they have that it does not, today

  • Raw simplex speed. About twice HiGHS's wall time on the Netlib medium tier, median per-instance ratio 2.0x to 2.1x across three runs an hour apart. HiGHS's pricing also takes fewer iterations.
  • MILP proof rate. 13 of 30 MIPLIB easy instances reach the published optimum in 60 s and 9 prove it. No mixed-integer rounding cuts, no cuts below the root, one core in the tree.
  • Very large LPs. Mittelmann's eight smallest instances: 0 of 8 inside 300 s. The interior point runs out of memory on the 100,000-row random model.
  • A GPU path. The CUDA backend exists as a pull request and has never been built or measured, because the team has had no GPU. The page does not say "GPU-accelerated" until a CSV exists.
  • Decades of tuning. CPLEX and Gurobi have it. This has four months.

Every row above points at a file in the repository that would have to change for the row to change. That is the design: claims that can be regenerated, not remembered.

04 · Inside

Four engines behind one call, chosen by the problem class.

solve(const Model&, const Options&) → Solution is the one entry point and the seam where later engines plug in. A model read from MPS, LP or QPS goes through presolve, is scaled, is dispatched by class, and comes back through postsolve to be re-measured against the original model before anything is written.

LP · default

Dual simplex

Bounded-variable revised simplex, sparse Markowitz LU with hyper-sparse solves, Devex pricing, product-form updates with a residual check. Produces a basis, so it is the node engine for branch-and-bound.

LP · primal

Primal simplex

Composite phase 1, no big-M. The hand-over engine when the dual's basis needs repair, and the reference the dual is measured against.

LP · opt-in

Interior point

Mehrotra predictor-corrector on normal equations through a from-scratch sparse LDLᵀ with an approximate-minimum-degree ordering written from the 1996 paper. The engine that scales; produces no basis.

LP · first-order

Restarted PDHG

Two sparse matrix-vector products and a projection per iteration, no serial dependency: the shape a GPU wants. Its answer is finished by the interior point from its own point. This is the GPU story.

MILP · MIQP

Branch and bound

Reliability branching with pseudocosts and strong branching, dive-then-best-bound, warm-started dual node LPs, root Gomory and lifted cover cuts (off by default, by measurement).

QP

Condat-Vũ primal-dual

Convexity decided by an LDLᵀ test before any arithmetic. A non-convex Hessian is refused with the negative pivot as the certificate, never solved to a local point and reported as global.

Verification as architecture

An exact rational oracle in the tests. An independent checker on every written answer. Certificates for the two verdicts that have no point to check. Postsolve re-measures the recovered point against the original model, so a wrong dual surfaces as a violation rather than as a quiet wrong number.

What a planner gets back

The objective, the primal point, the shadow price of every row, cost and right-hand-side ranging with the basis's degeneracy stated, an irreducible infeasible subsystem with witnesses when there is no plan, and a JSON stats blob. Exit codes mean something: 0 optimal, 1 a limit or a proven verdict, 3 unreadable, 5 numerical.

05 · The product

What it is at the end: an engine, three ways to call it, and the evidence beside it.

No GUI. The deliverable is the thing a refinery's planning tools would link against, plus the proof that it is right.

  1. The solver core, as a static library.

    LP, MILP, convex QP and MIQP behind one solve(). Presolve, scaling, four LP engines, branch-and-bound over LP and QP relaxations. Zero solver dependencies; fmt, CLI11, nlohmann/json and zlib are the whole list.

  2. A command line.

    sankhya solve model.mps --write-sol plan.sol --stats plan.json. Reads MPS, LP and QPS, including gzip. Writes the solution file with prices, ranges, certificates and the IIS; streams progress as JSON lines; stops cleanly on Ctrl-C with the best point so far.

  3. A C API and Python bindings.

    An FFI-safe surface with no C++ types crossing, and a Python package that is ctypes over it, so there is nothing to compile on the caller's side. Callbacks, interruption, and the same options table as the CLI.

  4. The independent verifier.

    A Python program that shares no code with the solver, checks every written answer against the original model, and is what the benchmark pass rates are counted by.

  5. The evidence, committed.

    Every CSV in bench/results/, the generated docs/BENCHMARKS.md, the provenance file, the SPDX bill of materials, and a reproduction script that takes a fresh clone to every claim. A judge with a laptop can regenerate the page you are reading.

  6. The GPU backend, when the card exists.

    The CUDA port of the first-order engine is written and unmeasured. The plan for the day hardware arrives is in docs/GPU_PLAN.md: intake, the order of three pull requests, the agreement test against the CPU path to 1e-9, and a published CPU-versus-GPU crossover including the region where the GPU loses. No speed-up is claimed before that table exists.

Demonstrated on refinery-shaped models

A crude-blending LP with a sulphur specification and a CDU throughput window, a QP variant with price impact, a unit-scheduling MILP, and a generated multi-period refinery planning model rolled out by the month, the day and the hour. Also power dispatch, supply chain and lot sizing from the case-study set.

06 · How to use it

Build once, then call it from the shell, from Python, or from C.

Requires CMake 3.20 or newer, Ninja and a C++20 compiler. The configure script picks a capable compiler rather than trusting PATH order, which matters on Windows machines carrying an old MinGW. No CUDA is needed for the CPU build.

Build and test

git clone https://github.com/thegoodengineers/SANKHYA
cd SANKHYA
scripts/configure.sh build Release
cmake --build build -j
ctest --test-dir build --output-on-failure

On Windows use Git Bash or MSYS2. scripts/preflight.sh names the two things that go wrong quietly there: a Microsoft Store python3 stub, and Smart App Control refusing a freshly linked binary.

Command line

./build/sankhya info  model.mps
./build/sankhya solve model.mps \
    --write-sol plan.sol --stats plan.json
./build/sankhya solve model.mps --ranging
./build/sankhya solve model.mps \
    --option algorithm=ipm --option time_limit=60
./build/sankhya solve model.mps \
    --progress-out progress.jsonl
./build/sankhya options

Reads MPS, free MPS, LP and QPS, gzipped or not. --option name=value takes any option in the table; options prints them all with defaults and the measurement behind each default.

Check the answer independently

python tools/verify_solution.py model.mps plan.sol

Re-derives feasibility, duality, integrality and the gap from the model and the solution file alone. Proves the Farkas certificate of an infeasible verdict and the ray of an unbounded one. Shares no code with the solver.

Exit codes

0 optimal · 1 a limit was hit, or infeasible / unbounded proven · 3 the file could not be read · 5 numerical or model error.

Python

export PYTHONPATH=bindings/python   # nothing to compile

import sankhya

m = sankhya.Model(maximize=True)
x = m.add_column(cost=3.0, upper=3.0, name="x")
y = m.add_column(cost=2.0, name="y")
m.add_row({x: 1.0, y: 1.0}, upper=4.0)
m.add_row({x: 1.0, y: 3.0}, upper=6.0)

r = m.solve()
print(r.status, r.objective, r.x)
# optimal 11.0 [3.0, 1.0]

m = sankhya.Model.read("data/netlib/afiro.mps")
r = m.solve(sankhya.Options(time_limit=30))
print(r.row_duals, r.primal_infeasibility)

The bindings are ctypes over the C API, so the shared library the build produces is the whole dependency. A failed call raises; an infeasible model is a successful call whose status says so. The result also carries the measured quality of the point, recomputed from the returned vectors rather than asserted by the engine.

C

#include <sankhya/sankhya.h>

sankhya_model*   m = sankhya_model_create();
sankhya_model_read(m, "model.mps");

sankhya_options* o = sankhya_options_create();
sankhya_options_set_double(o, "time_limit", 60.0);
sankhya_options_set_string(o, "algorithm", "dual-simplex");

sankhya_solution* s = NULL;
sankhya_solve(m, o, &s);
printf("%s %.10g\n",
       sankhya_solution_message(s),
       sankhya_solution_objective(s));

sankhya_solution_free(s);
sankhya_options_free(o);
sankhya_model_free(m);

No C++ types cross the boundary. sankhya_set_callback gets progress at most ten times a second and can stop the solve by returning non-zero; sankhya_model_interrupt does the same from another thread. An unknown option name is an error return, not a crash.

The options that matter

algorithmauto (the dual simplex), simplex, dual-simplex, pdhg, ipm
time_limitseconds; reaches inside the interior point's factorization
mip_relative_gap1e-4; a search that meets it reports optimal within the gap
presolve, scalingon by default; postsolve re-measures the point on the original model
enable_root_cutsoff by default, by measurement on MIPLIB
threads1; N runs the column loops under OpenMP with bit-identical results
pdhg_polishon; the interior point finishes the first-order answer from its own point
gpuwarns and falls back until a build carries the CUDA backend

Tolerances live in one header, include/sankhya/tolerances.hpp: primal and dual feasibility 1e-7, integrality 1e-6, pivot drop 1e-11. No magic numbers elsewhere.

The demo

demo/run_sih_demo.sh          # the PS26119 walkthrough
demo/run_sih_demo.sh --quick
demo/run_demo.sh --list
demo/run_demo.sh share2b      # solve, then verify

Walks the problem statement in its own order: blending LP with prices and ranges, the QP variant, the scheduling MILP corroborated by exhaustion, the three robustness hazards, the Netlib set with verification, the HiGHS comparison, and the list of what is not done. Every number it prints comes from a command it just ran. Offline.

What the solution file holds

Status and objective; the primal point; the shadow price of every row and the reduced cost of every column; cost and right-hand-side ranging when asked, with the basis's degeneracy stated; the certificate of an infeasible or unbounded verdict; the irreducible infeasible subsystem with one witness per element; the gap the run was asked for and the gap it achieved. All of it is what the verifier reads.

07 · What it does not do

Stated here, because a solver that is vague about its limits is not one a plant can plan around.

Not yetWhere it stands
GPU accelerationThe first-order engine it needs exists and is verified on CPU. The CUDA backend is a pull request that has never been compiled by the team, for lack of a GPU. --gpu warns and falls back.
Speed at Mittelmann size0 of 8 inside 300 s. The dual simplex's cost per iteration at 20,000 rows and above is the open engineering line, with per-phase timing committed and two of the three levers already pulled.
Branch-and-cut properRoot cuts exist; MIR cuts and cuts below the root do not. This is why MIPLIB proves 9 of 30.
CrossoverThe interior point's answer has no basis, so it cannot certify infeasibility or warm-start the tree. Pushing its point to a vertex is designed and not built.
Warm start through the APIThe dual simplex re-solves from a basis in a handful of pivots inside branch-and-bound; the public API does not expose it yet.
NLP and MINLPThe seam dispatches four problem classes cleanly. No nonlinear engine sits behind it.
Non-convex QPRefused deliberately, with a certificate. Reporting a local optimum as global is the one thing this solver will not do.
ParallelismOpenMP over the column loops, deterministic and bit-identical at 1 and 8 threads, and measured with no speed-up at Netlib scale. The tree runs on one core.
